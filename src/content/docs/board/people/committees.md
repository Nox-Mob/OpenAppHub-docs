Most boards have committees, like a finance committee, a nominating committee or an events committee. Each committee does one job and reports back to the board.

The **Committees** page keeps a list of your committees and who is on each one. That way everyone knows who to ask about what.

## Who can change committees

- The owner, administrators, the chair and the secretary can add and change committees.
- Everyone on the board can see them.

## Adding a committee

1. Select **Committees** in the side menu.
2. Type the **Committee name**, like _Finance committee_.
3. If you like, add its **Purpose**, like _Reviews the budget before each board meeting_. A purpose helps new board members understand what each committee does.
4. Select **Add committee**.

## Adding people to a committee

Under the committee, type the person's **Name** and their **Role**. The role can be anything, like _Chair_, _Secretary_ or _Member_. If you leave the role empty, it says _Member_. Then select add.

People on a committee **don't need an account**. You can list anyone, including people who aren't on the board. For example, a community volunteer who helps plan events can be on your events committee.

To take someone off a committee, select the remove button next to their name. To remove a whole committee and its list, select **Delete committee**.

## Good to know

- **A committee list is only a record.** Being on a committee doesn't give anyone rights in OpenBoardApp. What a person can do depends on their role. See [Members and terms](/board/people/members-and-terms/).
- **Committee meetings aren't separate meetings yet.** For now, add the committee's report as an agenda item at the board meeting, and take notes there.
