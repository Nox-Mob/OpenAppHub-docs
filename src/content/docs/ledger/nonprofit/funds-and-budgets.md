## Funds

A **fund** tracks money given for a specific purpose (a building fund, a scholarship), until the purpose is met. Money without a fund is unrestricted: the organization can use it for anything.

- Create a fund on **Funds** and mark it **restricted** when a donor limited how it may be used.
- Choose the fund on each gift and on each expense paid from it.
- When the purpose is met (the roof is fixed), **release** the money from its restriction; the release is recorded so your reports show it.
- The **Fund report** shows each fund's money in, money out, releases and balance.

:::note
Funds are tracked on transactions (like a tag with rules), not as separate sets of books.
:::

## Pledges

A **pledge** is a promised gift. It counts as income when promised, and is tracked until paid: record payments against it as they arrive, and see what's still outstanding on **Pledges**.

## Projects

A **project** is a piece of work with its own budget, like a renovation or an event. Choose the project on its transactions to compare budget and actual spending on **Projects**. Unlike a fund, a project doesn't restrict how money may be used.

## Budgets

On **Budgets**, set a budget for the year or for a month, account by account, then compare it to what actually happened.

## Categories and tags

- **Categories** add detail under an account (for example, Supplies split into Office and Events). Reports can group by category.
- **Tags** are flexible labels, several per transaction if you like (for example, *Annual gala*). Use them to find and filter transactions.

Archive any of these when you no longer use them; past transactions keep them.
